You worked the booth for four days. You collected a stack of business cards. A dozen Amazon sellers nodded, took samples, said "email me a quote." Then you sent the quote and heard nothing back. Not once. Ever.

It's not your product. It's not your price. It's the 72 hours after you shook hands.

## The window closes faster than you think

A trade-show lead is at peak temperature the moment they leave your booth. Then reality hits them. They flew home. Their inbox has 400 unread emails. They talked to 30 other suppliers who all said the same thing: "best quality, best price, we do everything." By the time your quote lands, they can't remember which booth was yours.

The buyer isn't rejecting you. They've simply lost the thread. And here's the part that stings: the supplier who follows up on day two, with something the buyer can actually verify, wins the deal you should have won.

## Western buyers vet before they reply

A US or EU Amazon seller is putting their capital, their listing, and their brand reputation in your hands. Before they answer a cold quote, they do what any careful operator does: they look you up.

Where do they look? Not your Alibaba page. LinkedIn.

Picture a seller back at their desk. They have your card and two others. They open LinkedIn and search each name. Two suppliers have real profiles, faces, posts about factory audits and shipping. The third, that's you, has nothing. A blank profile, or no profile at all. Which two get a reply?

The seller isn't being unfair. In their world, no LinkedIn footprint reads as risk. And risk loses to a boring supplier who looks accountable.

## The gap isn't your sourcing, it's your visibility

Here's the hard truth most sourcing agencies never hear: your factory relationships might be better than everyone else's at that show. Doesn't matter. If the buyer can't confirm you're real, established, and responsive without emailing you again, they move on. Silence is the safest choice for them.

The suppliers winning these deals aren't better at sourcing. They're just easier to trust from a browser tab.

## What actually keeps the lead warm

Three things close the gap between the handshake and the reply:

- **Follow up inside 48 hours.** Reference something specific from your conversation, the product category, a problem they mentioned. Prove you remember them, not just that you want an order.
- **Give them something to verify.** A LinkedIn profile with your face, your role, your company, and posts that show you handle QC, logistics, and problems. This is the check they run before replying, so pass it.
- **Make the next step obvious.** Don't end with "let me know." End with a specific, low-friction ask: a 15-minute call, a sample shipment, a spec sheet. Remove the thinking.

## The uncomfortable math

Every trade show costs you booth fees, flights, samples, and a week off the floor. If most of your leads go cold in three days, you're not paying for leads. You're paying to be forgotten.

The fix isn't a bigger booth or a lower price. It's being findable and credible in the exact 72-hour window when the buyer decides whether you're worth a reply. That's a presence problem, and presence can be built before the next show.
