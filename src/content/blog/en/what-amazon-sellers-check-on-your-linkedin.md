When a Western Amazon seller gets your quote, they don't reply first. They open LinkedIn and check you out. That thirty-second scan decides whether you get a conversation or silence.

Here's exactly what they look at, and how to pass each check.

## 1. Your headline

Your headline is the first line they read after your name. If it says "Sales Manager" or is blank, it tells them nothing. Say what you actually do for buyers like them.

*Do this:* "Helping Amazon sellers source [category] from vetted factories in [region]." Specific beats fancy.

## 2. Your About section

Buyers read this to answer one question: can this person solve my problem? A wall of company history doesn't. Lead with who you help, what you source, and how you handle the hard parts.

*Do this:* Three short paragraphs, written to the buyer, not about your factory.

## 3. Proof you handle QC and logistics

This is the check that separates real agencies from middlemen. Buyers want evidence you catch defects, manage inspections, and move goods, not just forward emails.

*Do this:* Post about a real inspection, a shipping problem you solved, a QC step you run. Show the work.

## 4. Your response signals

Buyers look for signs you're active and reachable. A profile last touched two years ago says you'll be slow when their shipment is stuck.

*Do this:* Post or engage regularly. An active profile signals an active partner.

## 5. Your company page

After your profile, they click your company. An empty or missing page makes the whole operation feel thin. A real page with a logo, description, and posts makes you look established.

*Do this:* Build the company page, link your profile to it, keep it current.

## 6. Mutual connections

If you share connections with the buyer, especially other sellers or logistics people, your trust score jumps. It's social proof they can verify without asking anyone.

*Do this:* Connect with sellers, freight forwarders, and Amazon-ecosystem people so overlaps appear naturally.

## 7. Your content

Buyers read a post or two to hear how you think. Do you understand their business, their margins, their Amazon problems? Or do you just push product?

*Do this:* Share practical insight on sourcing, tariffs, QC, and shipping. Teach, don't sell.

## 8. Endorsements and recommendations

A written recommendation from a Western client or partner is worth more than any brochure. It's a real person vouching that you deliver.

*Do this:* Ask satisfied clients for a short recommendation. One honest paragraph outperforms a page of self-praise.

## 9. A clear next step

Finally, they look for how to move forward. If your profile has no contact path and no obvious call to action, the momentum dies right there.

*Do this:* Make the next step obvious, a booked call, a message, a resource, so an interested buyer never has to guess.

## The pattern behind all nine

Notice what every check has in common: the buyer is trying to answer "is this supplier real, capable, and easy to work with?" without emailing you again. Your LinkedIn either answers yes or leaves them guessing, and a guessing buyer moves to the supplier who didn't make them wonder.

You don't need to be the cheapest. You need to be the one who's obviously safe to reply to.
